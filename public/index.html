Keenan Oliver is a South African designer and cultural producer based in Cape Town. He is the founder and creative director of Artefacts Office, an independent design office established in 2022. His practice has moved across graphic design, visual identity, publishing, image-making, art direction, film, digital work, exhibitions and spatial communication.

Oliver's career developed through a mixture of self-directed work, production environments, advertising and agency practice, and long-running relationships with artists, cultural organisations, brands and collaborators. His projects range from campaign and identity systems to books, images, exhibitions, hospitality spaces and cultural programmes. The relationship between making and documenting—how things are recorded, organised, circulated and remembered—recurs throughout the practice.

Early life and education

Cape Town and early visual culture

Oliver grew up in Cape Town, with Mitchells Plain forming an important part of the social and visual context of his early life. His relationship to design developed outside a straightforward graphic-design school-to-studio route. Music, popular culture, street life, photography, clothing and the visual language of everyday environments became part of a broader education in seeing and making.

Tygerberg Art Centre

Tygerberg Art Centre was where Oliver took graphic design as an extra high-school subject. The centre’s design programme brought together Design in Context, theory and technique, and practical Graphics and Printmaking, alongside illustration, jewellery, ceramics, fashion and interior studies. Its broader visual-arts programme included drawing, painting and photography. This combination of visual research, conceptual development and hands-on making shaped the way Oliver learned to move from an idea to an image, page or printed object.[7]

Marketing and self-directed learning

Oliver attended The Settlers High School and later completed coursework in marketing at Cape Peninsula University of Technology between 2013 and 2015, including marketing strategy, consumer behaviour, brand communication and market research. The training did not constitute a conventional graphic-design degree; it instead became part of a wider education that connected audience, positioning and communication to later visual work.

Career

Independent practice, 2017–2021

Oliver began working independently in 2017. Early projects included publication design such as Mambokadzi, visual research and treatments for directors and production companies, music-video art direction, social-media production and commercial film work. Freelance work and production roles exposed him to the practical conditions through which visual ideas are made: people, locations, schedules, materials, equipment, image capture, post-production and delivery.

His early production work included freelance art direction with 0307 Films, production assistance and social-media strategy with Andpeople on the Adidas AREA3 project, and production or art-department assistance with Monkey Films, Juice Films and Carbon Films. These experiences contributed to a practice that moves naturally between conceptual development, design and production.

Independent projects and collaborations

Alongside the work associated with Autodidacts & Associates, Oliver developed a parallel body of small-scale independent projects and collaborations. These included Still Not Joshy Pascoe, an image-led self-publishing project that explored the significance of ordinary photographs, social-media exchanges and improvised editorial forms, as well as work associated with the AREA3 programme and its emphasis on co-creation, access and design within Cape Town's youth culture.

These projects were comparatively informal, but they were important to the development of a practice in which publishing, photography, design and cultural research could exist together without a strict separation between disciplines.

Autodidacts & Associates

Work with Autodidacts & Associates became a formative period in Oliver's independent practice. The collective brought together graphic design, photography, fashion, art direction and cultural commentary, often through projects connected to Cape Town and Mitchells Plain.

The KAAS headwear project combined clothing, photography, styling and place. Oliver worked on photography, art direction and design, with styling by Lubabalo Nkobo and assistance from Kaylynne Damonze. Coverage of the project described the work as contributing to a narrative from within Mitchells Plain rather than replacing an existing narrative, a position that became important to Oliver's developing approach to authorship and documentation.[1]

“This is not so much about changing the narrative coming out of Mitchells Plain and more so about contributing to it by defining my own narrative.”— Keenan Oliver, quoted in Bubblegum Club's coverage of KAAS.[1]

Earlier work around Leaf Apparel similarly treated documentation as part of the work rather than an afterthought. Oliver's contribution to the resulting archive centred on capturing process, people and making, and on the idea that self-documentation affects how a community's history is later understood.[2]

Advertising and agency practice

Alongside independent work, Oliver developed experience within larger commercial and agency environments. At 99c Advertising & Communications, where he worked as a DTP Art Director from 2021 to 2023, he worked across print production including magazines, posters, packaging and large-format applications. The role developed his understanding of production systems, technical consistency and the relationship between visual direction and final artwork.

At VML South Africa, from 2023 to 2024, he worked as a Senior Interactive Designer and Art Director. His work included digital campaigns and social-media systems for SuperSport football coverage, translating broader campaign strategy into visual concepts and platform-specific communication.

In 2024, Oliver was Design Lead at 5PM Studio for the BMW Art Generation programme at Nirox Sculpture Park. The role involved creative direction, visual identity, print and digital systems, and directing design through research, concept development and production.

He subsequently worked as an Art Director and Design Lead at Iconic Creative Agency in 2025 and 2026. His work there included creative and design direction for NBA Africa campaigns, Red Bull Shay'i Moto, Netflix South Africa and New Balance.

Cultural work and publishing

A parallel strand of Oliver's practice developed through cultural projects in which design was not confined to a single visual identity or communication format. Books, exhibitions, hospitality spaces, photography and institutional projects allowed him to work with questions of memory, place, authorship and cultural circulation at different scales.

Babbi Deli & Bar

Babbi Deli & Bar was a significant project in the development of Oliver's practice. He was a founding partner responsible for its brand architecture and visual direction, extending his work from graphic systems into a physical cultural environment.

Babbi operated as a deli, listening bar and social space on Bree Street in Cape Town. Contemporary coverage described it as a small listening bar in an inconspicuous alleyway, known for food, wine, records and later live music, and characterised by an intentionally low-key presence in the city.[3] For Oliver, the project required an understanding of identity as something experienced through a room, its objects, signals, rhythms and relationships, rather than through printed material alone.

The significance of Babbi within the broader practice lies in this shift of scale. A visual identity could become an environment, and an environment could become part of a city's cultural memory. The project helped establish a continuing interest in how design can organise experience and create conditions for culture to gather.

Lost Libraries, Burnt Archives

Lost Libraries, Burnt Archives was produced in response to the 2021 fire at Jagger Library at the University of Cape Town and the resulting loss and damage to material in the African Studies Special Collection. The publication brought together 22 contributors working across short stories, artworks, poems and essays.[4]

Oliver designed the book, working within a project concerned with archives, destruction and the question of what emerges from the remains of an institution's memory. The physical edition was handmade by Pulp Paperworks, with 100 copies distributed to libraries, research institutions and specialist archives, while an electronic edition was made available more broadly.[4]

The project made explicit a concern that had been present in earlier work: documentation is not neutral. The act of deciding what to record, how to organise it and how to make it available can influence what survives as cultural knowledge.

We Proceed in the Footsteps of the Sunlight

For Ghanaian-German artist Zohra Opoku's first museum survey exhibition at Zeitz MOCAA, We Proceed in the Footsteps of the Sunlight, Oliver designed the exhibition identity and accompanying monograph. The exhibition traces a decade of Opoku's work across textiles, photography, printmaking and installation, with themes including memory, cultural inheritance and personal history.[5]

The publication extends the exhibition into another form. Its task was not simply to catalogue objects but to establish relationships between images, texts and material references. Oliver worked with sequence, typography, scale and physical production to translate the exhibition's concerns into a book that could carry its own rhythm and argument. Zeitz MOCAA identifies the monograph as designed by Oliver.[6]

Other cultural commissions

Oliver's wider cultural work has included production design for Tony Gum's Milked in Africa, presented at Fotografiska in New York; visual identity and packaging for Koleka Putuma's Can I Get a Witness? through Manyano Media; and production design for Urucu Media's B(L)IND THE SACRIFICE. His institutional work has also included projects associated with Standard Bank Arts, FNB Art Joburg and the Centre for African Studies at the University of Basel.

These projects span exhibition, publishing, film, institutional communication and cultural infrastructure. They also illustrate a recurring expansion in scale: from the individual image or page, to systems, publications, rooms and institutional contexts.

Artefacts Office

Oliver established Artefacts Office in 2022 as an independent design office through which the different strands of his practice could exist within a shared structure. The studio operates across identity, publishing, exhibition, digital, film and spatial communication, working with artists, cultural institutions, organisations and brands.

The formation of the office was not a departure from Oliver's earlier practice so much as a way of giving that practice a more durable structure. The independent work, agency experience, production knowledge and cultural projects accumulated over the preceding years could now be organised within a studio whose output is broader than any single medium.

Practice and approach

Across Oliver's work, a recurring concern is the relationship between making and recording. A book, image, identity, room or exhibition can operate as a record of a moment, but it can also shape how that moment is later encountered and remembered.

Perspective is therefore central to the practice. Oliver has described the way a person sees as something formed through experience, memory, taste, relationships and place. The aim is not to make every project autobiographical, but to ensure that the work contains a specific point of view rather than a generic design solution.

This perspective helps explain why the practice moves between disciplines. Graphic design, photography, publishing, art direction, film and spatial work are treated less as isolated services than as tools within a larger process of giving form to information, experience and cultural material.

Across these fields, Oliver is interested in how design can represent, organise, document and circulate cultural material—especially work that might otherwise remain temporary, fragmented or difficult to access.
